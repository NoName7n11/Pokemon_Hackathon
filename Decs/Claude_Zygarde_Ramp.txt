****** Design notes (built for a GREEDY BOT, not a human) ******

Problem being solved: the Hydrapple list loses by never coming online.
Measured on ladder -- wins average 6.0 turns, losses average 17.8 turns; one
loss had ZERO attacks across 13 turns. Two Stage-2 lines (Applin->Dipplin->
Hydrapple ex, Chikorita->Bayleef->Meganium) need 2 evolution steps before any
damage happens.

Fix: every attacker is a BASIC. Zero evolution steps, so the deck cannot brick
on missing evolution pieces.

Why Mega Zygarde ex (1056) as the primary:
  - Basic, so it is NOT subject to "evolving into a Mega ends your turn"
    (POKEMON_RULES.md sec.12). Basic Megas skip that cost entirely.
  - 310 HP -- the tankiest drawback-free attacker in the legal pool.
  - Gaia Wave: 3 Energy, 200 damage, and the rider is pure UPSIDE
    (takes 30 less next turn). No self-damage, no Energy discard, no
    next-turn lockout.

The drawback-text rule (the key bot constraint):
  main.py's _best_attack_index ranks attacks by PRINTED DAMAGE ONLY -- it never
  parses effect text. So any attack whose text punishes repeated use is a trap.
  Rejected on this basis:
    - Mega Mawile ex (695): 260 dmg, but base drops to 30 if the target already
      has damage counters. The bot would spam a 30-damage attack.
    - Gouging Fire ex (46): 260 dmg, then cannot use it again until it leaves play.
    - Koraidon ex / Latias ex / Yveltal ex / Zacian ex: next-turn attack lockouts.
    - Pikachu ex (328) / Black Kyurem ex (179): self-damage.
    - Iron Boulder (971): 170 dmg but does NOTHING unless hand sizes match.
    - Mega Diancie ex (766): damage scales with Energy the bot must discard itself.

Support Pokémon:
  - Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex (117): 210 HP Basic backup. Demolish is
    {F} + 2 Colorless, so it is the easiest attack in the deck to pay for.
    Damage ignores Weakness/Resistance.
  - Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon (386): 110 HP, 1 Prize only. Rock Kagura costs 1
    Energy and deals 0 damage -- which means with a single Energy attached it is
    the ONLY legal attack, so the greedy agent picks it and it ramps
    (searches a Basic {F} Energy out of the deck and attaches it).
  - Regirock ex (447): 230 HP wall; Regi Charge pulls 2 {F} back from the discard.

Energy math (the point of the deck):
  Attach 1/turn only        -> Gaia Wave online turn 3.
  Attach + Crispin/Waitress -> online turn 2.
  Compare Hydrapple, which needed two evolution steps AND energy before
  threatening anything.

Search note: Poké Pad cannot find Rule Box Pokémon, so it cannot fetch Mega
Zygarde ex -- it is in the list to find Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon (386). Ultra
Ball is the only search that reaches the Mega ex attackers, hence 4 copies.

****** MEASURED RESULT: THIS DECK LOSES. DO NOT ADOPT AS-IS. ******

  deck_head2head.py Zygarde_Ramp Hydrapple 50 (100 seat-balanced games, one agent
  piloting both sides):
      Zygarde_Ramp 37/100 = 37.0%  [95% CI 28.2%-46.8%]  avg 135 steps
      => Hydrapple BETTER (CI upper bound is below 50%)

  Experiment 1 -- was the agent's _prize_value(megaEx)=3 suppressing it?
      Monkeypatched to the rulebook value of 2 and re-ran the same matchup:
      23/100 = 23.0% [15.8%-32.2%]. WORSE, not better. Hypothesis REFUTED.
      Making the agent more willing to lead with Zygarde made it lose harder,
      which means Zygarde in the Active slot is feeding Prizes, not winning races.

  Experiment 2 -- tempo probe, 12 mirror games per deck:
      Zygarde_Ramp  first attack turn 3.7, 6.9 attacks/game, 627 printed dmg/game, 21.2 turns
      Hydrapple     first attack turn 4.0, 4.8 attacks/game, 142 printed dmg/game, 14.9 turns

      The design thesis WORKED on its own terms -- this deck attacks earlier, more
      often, and for 4.4x more printed damage. It still loses. So "comes online
      slowly" was NOT the binding constraint in this matchup, and the ladder
      diagnosis (6-turn wins vs 17.8-turn losses) does not translate into
      "more damage sooner => more wins".

  Best remaining explanation -- PRIZE ECONOMY, the thing this build got wrong:
      Zygarde_Ramp: 10 of 14 Pokémon are Rule Box (4 Mega Zygarde ex + 4
      Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon ex + 2 Regirock ex). Almost every KO against us
      hands over 2 Prizes, so the opponent needs only ~3 KOs.
      Hydrapple: 13 of 21 Pokémon are non-ex bodies that cost only 1 Prize, so it
      can absorb KOs while trading.
      Raw damage does not matter if each of our KOs pays double.

  Also suspect: Cornerstone Mask Ogerpon (386) Rock Kagura is a 0-damage attack.
  It was included as a ramp trick, but _best_attack_index picks the highest
  printed damage among LEGAL attacks -- so whenever only 1 Energy is attached,
  the agent attacks for literally 0. Average damage per attack here is 91,
  well under Gaia Wave's 200, which says the deck spends a lot of attacks on
  Rock Kagura and Mountain Ramming rather than on Zygarde.

  NEXT ITERATION would: cut the Rule Box count hard (single-Prize attackers as
  the default body), drop the 0-damage ramp attack, and only then re-test.

KNOWN RISKS (identified during design, before the above measurements):
  1. Prize math. Mega ex gives up 2 Prizes per KO (POKEMON_RULES.md sec.12), so
     3 KOs loses the game. 310 HP is the mitigation, not a guarantee.
  2. main.py's _prize_value returns 3 for megaEx, but the rulebook says Mega = 2.
     If that is wrong, the agent OVER-penalizes its own Zygarde
     (_live_attacker_score prize_penalty, _eval_state ko_risk) and may avoid
     leading with the card the deck is built around. Verify before adopting.
  3. Weakness to {G}. Our own previous deck was Grass; if Grass is common on
     ladder this is a real matchup tax.
  4. Zygarde retreat 2 -- hence 3 Switch + 3 Air Balloon.
