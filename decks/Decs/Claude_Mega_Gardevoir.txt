****** Concept ******

Built on request around Mega Gardevoir ex (747). The combo is real:

  Mega Symphonia costs ONE {P} Energy and does 50 damage for EACH {P} Energy
  attached to ALL of your Pokémon. Damage scales off the whole board, not off
  the attacker -- so the attacker never needs to be loaded up.

Support chosen to feed that multiplier:
  - Smoochum (183) "Delightful Kiss" costs ZERO Energy and pulls 2 Basic {P}
    Energy out of the deck onto a Benched Pokémon. Free ramp that directly adds
    100 damage to every future Mega Symphonia.
  - Rare Candy (1079) skips Kirlia entirely (Ralts -> Mega Gardevoir ex).
  - Buddy-Buddy Poffin (1086) fetches Ralts (70 HP) and Smoochum (30 HP); both
    are under the 70 HP cap.
  - Wondrous Patch (1146) returns {P} Energy from discard to a Benched {P} Pokémon.
  - Scream Tail ex (969), 190 HP Basic with a flexible {P} + 2 Colorless cost,
    holds the Active slot while the Stage 2 assembles.
  - Mega Gardevoir ex is 360 HP -- the largest body in the legal pool.


****** MEASURED RESULT: THIS DECK LOSES. NOT ADOPTED. ******

deck_head2head.py vs Hydrapple, 100 seat-balanced games, one agent piloting both:

  v1 (support-heavy: Mesprit/Cresselia/4 Smoochum)  19/100 = 19.0% [12.5-27.8%]
  v2 (those cut for 4 Scream Tail ex)               20/100 = 20.0% [13.3-28.9%]

Changing the deck composition did not move it. The cause is not the card list.


****** ROOT CAUSE: THE AGENT CANNOT SEE THIS CARD'S DAMAGE ******

The engine reports Attack.damage = 0 for Mega Symphonia, because "50x" variable
damage is stored as zero. main.py ranks every attacker by that single field
(_best_attack_index, _best_usable_damage). So the agent believes Mega Gardevoir
ex is a zero-damage Pokémon and will not promote it or lead with it.

Attack-selection probe, 10 games:
  Active-slot occupancy   Scream Tail ex 334 steps  vs  Mega Gardevoir ex 55 steps
The agent parked on the card it could read (printed 120) over the one that
actually hits for 200-400.

Scope of the blind spot (whole card pool, not just this deck):
  110 attacks have variable "50x"-style damage
  381 of 1556 engine attacks report damage == 0 -- all tied in the ranking
  391 of 1811 attack rows (22%) have real value that printed damage misses
      (variable 110, coin-flip 107, discards own Energy 63, next-turn lockout 61,
       self-damage 49, conditional 27)


****** HOW MUCH IS DECK vs AGENT? (4 arms x 100 games vs Hydrapple) ******

Hydrapple always piloted by the STOCK agent; only the Gardevoir side varied:

  stock   unmodified main.agent                                21.0% [14.2-30.0]
  dmg     Mega Symphonia estimated dynamically as
          50 x (all {P} Energy in play)                        34.0% [25.5-43.7]
  bench   dmg + attach Energy to the Bench, not the Active     40.0% [30.9-49.8]
  full    bench + force Gardevoir into the Active slot         38.0% [29.1-47.8]

  => +19 points, ~2x the win rate, from AGENT changes with the deck untouched.

Notes on those arms:
  - The variable-damage estimate is a GENERAL fix and belongs in main.py
    regardless of which deck ships; it is not specific to Gardevoir.
  - Bench-first Energy attachment is DECK-SPECIFIC. It is right here (Mega
    Symphonia counts Energy everywhere, and Bench Energy survives a KO on the
    attacker) and probably wrong for Hydrapple, which wants its Active powered.
  - Forcing Gardevoir Active did NOT help (38% vs 40%, overlapping CIs). The gain
    came from the agent EVALUATING the card correctly, not from steering it.


****** NOT CHASED ******

The agent still never uses Overflowing Wishes (also 0 printed damage), which is
this deck's actual ramp engine: +1 {P} Energy to EACH Benched Pokémon, straight
from the deck. A fuller deck-aware agent would ramp with it on a wide bench and
then swing, so 40% is probably not the true ceiling.

Not pursued because Codex_FireCamerupt reaches 66% vs Hydrapple under the STOCK
agent -- a better deck with no agent work beats this deck with a lot of it.
The variable-damage fix is still worth taking on its own merits.
